The systemic biology wall

Pharma is spending heavily on AI for discovery. Candidates designed with it clear Phase I at high rates and still fail in Phase II at the rate they always did. The models learn from fragmented, single-target data with no whole-body physiology in it. We call that missing layer the systemic data gap, and the wall in Phase II is where it shows.

{% include figures/wall.html %}

An isolated cell has no body

A cell in a dish cannot clear a drug through a liver, carry it through blood vessels or stop it at a blood-brain barrier. A 2D screen therefore misses the liabilities that appear when organs interact.

Standard models are slow and not scalable

Rodent studies capture systemic effects, after 18 to 24 months and grams of synthesised compound. Organoids need months to mature and still model one tissue.

A black box cannot be audited

If a black-box candidate fails or hits an off-target, nobody can trace the mechanism or the decision path. Pharma teams and regulators need both.
