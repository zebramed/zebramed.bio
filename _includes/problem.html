The systemic biology wall

Most clinical failures come down to efficacy or safety. Both depend on how a living body responds to a drug, yet standard preclinical models, from cell screens to purely computational AI, never see one. Candidates designed in silico clear Phase I, then hit the same Phase II failure rates the industry has faced for decades.

ZebraMed introduces the missing systemic layer: physical whole-organism data translated into auditable human insight.

Cells have no body

Cell cultures in a dish cannot metabolise a compound through a liver, clear it through kidneys or test a blood-brain barrier. They cannot model a seizure or a vascular collapse. Single-tissue assays miss the systemic liabilities that only emerge when organs interact.

Standard models are slow and not scalable

Rodent studies capture systemic effects, after 18 to 24 months and grams of synthesised compound. Organoids need months to mature and still model one tissue.

A black box cannot be audited

Most in silico models are black boxes. If a candidate fails or hits an off-target, nobody can trace the mechanism or the decision path. Pharma teams and regulators need auditable evidence.
