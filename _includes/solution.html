The automated biofoundry

The transparent zebrafish larva is a whole-organism vertebrate model small enough for automated microfluidic screening. Sharing 82% of human disease-associated genes, it possesses functional organs including an active liver, heart, nervous system, and blood-brain barrier. Our biofoundry standardises high-throughput data generation, using FishEye computer vision to capture multi-organ morphology and behavioural phenotypes at scale. Larvae are used at stages not protected under EU Directive 2010/63/EU, and the pipeline is built as a New Approach Methodology in line with the FDA Modernization Act 2.0.

The cross-species systemic atlas

A proprietary multi-species knowledge graph mapping organs, physiological functions, and molecular signalling pathways into a unified systemic network. Whole-body larval perturbation data is enriched with human pathobiology and aligned with clinical outcomes through genetic orthology and adverse outcome pathways. The atlas ingests client in vitro, organoid, or rodent data, placing isolated bench readouts into whole-body context to improve translational accuracy.

Reason

XenoEx: interpretable reasoning

XenoEx reasons over the atlas in auditable steps that research teams can inspect and redirect. It scores candidates for multi-organ toxicity and efficacy, re-evaluates stalled assets, and ranks target hypotheses along complete mechanistic chains. When its confidence on a molecule falls under the threshold agreed with the client, XenoEx autonomously designs a targeted validation run and dispatches it to the biofoundry, updating the atlas once physical data returns.
