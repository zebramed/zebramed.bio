The platform

A biofoundry produces whole-organism data. The translation graph links it, and the data clients bring, to human biology, and XenoEx reasons over the graph to decide what to run next.

Run

The automated biofoundry

Transparent zebrafish larvae share 82% of human disease-associated genes and carry a working liver, heart, nervous system and blood-brain barrier. ZebraChip microfluidics load each larva and hold it in a fixed lateral position; FishEye computer vision reads morphology and behaviour. We built the pipeline for the New Approach Methodology framework of the FDA Modernization Act 2.0.

Translate

The Systemic Translation Graph

The graph organises what we know into one structure. Its core is zebrafish data: changes in gene expression, linked to the phenotypes and behaviours observed with them. Orthology, shared ontologies and OECD adverse outcome pathways connect each link to its human counterpart, step by mechanistic step. Data from a client's own models, such as cell lines, organoids or other species, is placed in the same graph, so its results can be read in human terms.

Reason

XenoEx, interpretable multi-agent AI

XenoEx reasons over the graph in steps a scientist can read, audit and redirect. It humanises signatures, scores candidates for systemic efficacy and multi-organ toxicity, re-evaluates shelved assets and ranks target hypotheses. When its confidence falls under the client's agreed threshold, it designs the validation run and sends it to the biofoundry.
