Redrawing the Systemic Translation Graph

Round 2: fuller versions of A and B, where every node says what it is. Same rules as the other plates (420 wide, no text under 12.5px, mint for what XenoEx is reading, green for human terms); they run taller. Round 1, with C and the current figure, is collapsed at the bottom.

What the figure has to say

  • It is organised knowledge, not the dataset: links between gene-expression changes and the phenotypes and behaviours seen with them.
  • The links follow mechanistic chains, step by step.
  • Three kinds of bridge reach human biology: orthology, shared ontologies, adverse outcome pathways.
  • Zebrafish is the core; a client's own data (cell lines, organoids, other species) is placed in the same graph and read in human terms.

Proposal A2

The chain, in full

Same reading as A (client, zebrafish, human; gene to organism), with the parts A left out.

What changed

  • Nodes say what they are. Shape gives the type (gene, phenotype, behaviour). Zebrafish nodes carry a direction and the age they were seen at. Human nodes carry their vocabulary (HGNC, HPO) and their role in the pathway (key event, adverse outcome).
  • Edges say what they mean ("increases", "precedes"), not only that two things are linked.
  • Convergence: two gene changes feed one cell event, so it reads as a network, not a line.
  • A fourth level, the organism, with a behaviour (swimming). The organoid now covers up to the organ, so the stripe only the zebrafish reaches is visible.
  • The AOP is drawn as its own chain on the human side, ending in the adverse outcome, with a bracket naming it.

Risks

  • Twice the content of A; it needs the caption to explain dpf, KE and AO.
  • For your team to check: cyp3a and fasn up → lipid → liver size; swimming ↓ ↔ fatigue is the weakest mapping.

Proposal B2

The neighbourhood, with one node opened

Same idea as B (the graph as XenoEx reads it), with real structure and one node shown in full.

What changed

  • One node opened in a card: what it is, when, how it was measured, how many larvae, how big the effect. That makes "nodes with meanings" literal: each one is a record, not a dot.
  • Two branches, liver and heart, from one exposure. That is the systemic point a cell assay cannot make.
  • Typed nodes everywhere, faint ones included (dot, ring, diamond), so the background reads as more of the same knowledge, not decoration.
  • Edges carry relations ("decreases", "precedes"), and the AOP step runs on the human side, from hepatomegaly (key event) to liver injury (adverse outcome).
  • Human terms tagged with their vocabulary and role, as in A2.

Risks

  • The card adds text. On the site it could later open on tap for any node, but that is more build work; static, it shows one.
  • For your team to check: kcnh2a ↓ → heart rate ↓ → bradycardia (KCNH2 is hERG, the standard cardiac-safety gene); startle ↓ ↔ startle reflex.
Round 1: A, B, C and the current figure

Proposal A

The chain: one mechanism, step by step

One worked example, read across biological levels. Three rows: what the client's model can see, what the zebrafish adds, what each step means in human terms.

Why

  • Draws your definition almost literally: a gene change linked to a phenotype, the mechanistic chain, and each kind of bridge named exactly where it applies.
  • Shows client data placed by level. A cell line stops at the cell; the organ step, in a living body, is the zebrafish's contribution. That is the Problem section's argument ("an isolated cell has no body"), made visible.
  • Toxicologists and regulators recognise the AOP shape: molecular event, key events, adverse outcome.

Speaks to

Pharma scientists, safety and toxicology teams, NAM reviewers.

Risks

  • Reads as one example rather than a graph; the tidy rows can feel like a slide.
  • The biology is fictional but has to be plausible: your team should check the chain (cyp3a → lipid build-up → liver size; CYP3A4 → steatosis → liver injury).

Proposal B

The path: the graph as XenoEx reads it

The graph's texture in the background, with one lit path from a client's result, through the zebrafish core, across a bridge into human terms. Gene, phenotype and behaviour each appear once, and so does each bridge.

Why

  • Makes "graph" visible: nodes and edges, many of them unlabelled, which says there is more than the example.
  • Connects to the next plate: XenoEx's decision trace is a walk along exactly this kind of path. It also matches the hero loop's "structure" station.
  • Closest to the current figure, so the least redesign; keeps the mint-is-live rule used across the site.

Speaks to

AI-literate investors, data and ML partners.

Risks

  • Faint nodes can read as decoration, the generic "AI network" picture.
  • The busiest of the three on a phone (ten labels).

Proposal C

The hub: any model in, human out

Client models on the left land in a zebrafish core; on the right, three kinds of human-relevant output, each labelled with the bridge that produces it.

Why

  • Tells the commercial story in one look: clients keep their own models and get answers in human terms.
  • Zebrafish is literally the core, and the three bridges become the vocabulary of the offer (genes, phenotypes, outcomes).
  • Names no specific biology, so there is the least to fact-check.

Speaks to

Investors, business development, a client's head of R&D.

Risks

  • The most abstract: a scientist learns little from an unlabelled core.
  • Hub diagrams are common in pitch decks; it may look like everyone else's.

Now

The current figure

Drawn for the old definition: a graph of zebrafish states with every link carried across the same way.

What it misses

  • One kind of bridge for everything; orthology, ontologies and AOPs are not told apart.
  • No client data, so "any model in" is invisible.
  • No direction: nothing reads as a mechanistic chain.
  • Colour means node type here (teal organ, green behaviour), while everywhere else on the site mint means "live" and green means "result".
